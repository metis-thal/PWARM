# Golden Experiment — Mission 001: Discover Gravity

> This is PWARM's **golden experiment**: the most complete, most auditable
> record of one discovery. Every future mission aims for this documentation
> standard. Everything below is regenerated by
> `bash reproducibility/mission_001/run.sh`.

---

## 1. Scientific Question

Can an artificial agent discover a physical law — without being told that it
exists, what form it takes, or what its value is — using only experiments it
designs itself and measurements it is allowed to observe?

Operationalized for Mission 001: determine the gravitational acceleration of
an unknown world from free-fall observations.

## 2. Hidden Variables

| Variable | Value | Who may know it |
|----------|-------|-----------------|
| `gravity` | 9.81 m/s² | physics engine only (`universe_001/config.yaml`, `hidden: true`) |

The AI-facing *manifest* lists the NAME `gravity` — knowing what you don't
know is the legitimate starting point of the method. The VALUE is loaded only
physics-side (`pymo.universes` → `Laboratory`) and is structurally invisible
to `ScientistAgent` (see `docs/architecture/ai-physics-contract.md`).

## 3. Available Instruments

One instrument: a **drop rig** — release a 1.0 kg sphere from a chosen height,
record `(t, z)` until contact. The planner v1 designs drops at heights
**10 m, 20 m, 5 m** (`src/pymo/scientist/planner.py`).

## 4. Experimental Constraints

- The AI may only propose experiments; physics executes them
  (`ExperimentSpec` in, `ObservationRecord` out).
- No parameter may be set from hidden values; heights are free design choices.
- Verification requires independent agreement: cross-experiment relative
  spread ≤ 0.001 and confidence ≥ 0.999 (`Mission` acceptance fields).

## 5. AI Observation Space

Per experiment: a `MeasurementRecord` of time and height samples
(`t: float [s]`, `z: float [m]`), plus the derived velocity channel the
laboratory exposes. Nothing else — no accelerations precomputed, no truth.
Raw as-run samples: [`results/observations.json`](../../reproducibility/mission_001/results/observations.json).

## 6. Experiment Selection

`ExperimentPlanner.propose` returns three drops at *different* heights.
Design rationale (strategy, not physics): a law that survives independent
conditions is a law; a single fit is an anecdote. Established laws are not
re-discovered — if the knowledge base already holds `gravity` at sufficient
confidence, the planner proposes nothing.

## 7. Raw Observations

Three trajectories, ~90–180 samples each, e.g. `drop_h10_m1`:
z descends 9.43 m in the first 0.33 s, 7.77 m at 0.67 s — the free-fall
parabola. Full arrays in
[`results/observations.json`](../../reproducibility/mission_001/results/observations.json)
(AI-side artifacts; ground truth never appears there).

## 8. Hypothesis

Each record is fitted with a quadratic `z(t) = z0 + v0 t − ½ g t²`
(`fit_free_fall`), yielding one estimate per experiment:

| Experiment | Estimate | R² |
|------------|----------|-----|
| `drop_h10_m1` | g ≈ 9.80999 m/s² | 1.0000 |
| `drop_h20_m1` | g ≈ 9.80999 m/s² | 1.0000 |
| `drop_h5_m1`  | g ≈ 9.81000 m/s² | 1.0000 |

## 9. Verification

`verify()` cross-checks independent estimates: relative spread 0.0000% across
three experiments, mean R² = 1.0, confidence ≥ 0.999 → **stable**. Three
agreements turn three measurements into one law.

## 10. Ground Truth

g = 9.81 m/s² (`universe_001/config.yaml`). Read ONLY on the evaluation side
(this document, `scientific_evaluation/`, CI's reproducibility test). The AI
never receives it.

## 11. Error

measured 9.80999 m/s² vs truth 9.81 m/s² → **relative error 0.0001%**.

## 12. Reproducibility

- Envelope: [`reproducibility/mission_001/`](../../reproducibility/mission_001/)
  (`config.yaml` · `seed.txt` · `run.sh` · `expected_output.txt` · `results/`)
- Determinism: no RNG anywhere in the AI↔physics loop; numpy seeded to 0 as
  belt-and-braces (`reproducibility/mission_001/seed.txt`).
- CI runs the envelope on every push (Ubuntu + Windows) and asserts every
  published hypothesis sits within threshold of the hidden truth
  (`tests/test_reproducibility.py`).
- Knowledge persistence: a second run with the same knowledge file concludes
  from civilization knowledge with zero experiments (`pwarm mission run 001
  --knowledge knowledge/universe_001.json`).
